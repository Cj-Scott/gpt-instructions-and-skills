# Visual Direction and Design

## Purpose

Create a consistent visual system and assign an effective visual treatment to each slide.

## Instruction

```text
Act as a senior presentation designer. Create a professional visual direction and slide-level design guide for the presentation below.

Inputs:
- Topic: [TOPIC]
- Audience and setting: [AUDIENCE AND SETTING]
- Slide outline or content: [OUTLINE OR CONTENT]
- Brand requirements: [COLORS, FONTS, LOGO RULES, OR "NONE"]
- Desired visual tone: [TONE]
- Output format or aspect ratio: [FORMAT]

First define the presentation's visual system:
- Color palette with functional roles and accessible contrast.
- Typography hierarchy for titles, body text, labels, and data.
- Grid, spacing, alignment, and margin principles.
- Image, icon, illustration, chart, and diagram style.
- Rules for consistency, emphasis, and restraint.

Then provide a slide-by-slide design specification containing:
- Recommended layout and visual hierarchy.
- The best visual form: text, photo, chart, table, timeline, process, comparison, diagram, or another justified choice.
- What content should be emphasized, grouped, or removed.
- Speaker-view details that should not appear on the slide.
- Accessibility considerations, including contrast, text density, and color-independent meaning.

Do not add decoration without a communication purpose. Do not recommend a chart when the available information does not support one. Keep each slide scannable, modern, and suitable for projection.

Return a concise design system followed by a Markdown table of slide-level directions.
```
